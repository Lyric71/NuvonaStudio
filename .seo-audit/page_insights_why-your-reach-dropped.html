LinkedIn Strategy Algorithm Reach 29 March 2026

Why your reach dropped 50%+
in 2026
(and no, it isn't your content)

LinkedIn organic reach is down 50% since 2023. The cause isn't your writing. It's a new algorithm called 360Brew. Here's what changed under the hood, and the moves that actually work now.

By Nuvora Studio · 8 min read

LinkedIn scrapped its entire ranking system.

A new AI called 360Brew now decides who sees what.

Your impressions fell off a cliff. Posts that pulled thousands of views six months ago sit at a few hundred today. You didn't get worse at writing. LinkedIn replaced the machine that decides who reads your posts.

In late 2025, LinkedIn retired its old content engine and rolled out 360Brew. A 150-billion-parameter AI model, built internally by the company's Foundation AI Technologies team. Think of it as a robotic editor that reads every post on the platform, decides whether you actually know what you're talking about, and cross-checks your profile before it lets anybody else see what you wrote.

The impact landed immediately, and it's measurable.

"Views are down by 50%, engagement has dropped by 25%, and follower growth is down by 59%." (Richard van der Blom, Algorithm Insights 2025 Report)

Volume used to win. That trade is over.

Now LinkedIn tests every post on a small audience before it gets any real distribution.

Under the old system, LinkedIn pushed your post to a wide slice of your network. If enough people reacted, it snowballed. You could get lucky with mediocre content on a good day.

360Brew killed that lottery. Every post now lands on a small test group first, usually 2 to 5% of your connections. The system watches what those people actually do. Not whether they tap a like button. Whether they stop scrolling, read to the bottom, and do something meaningful afterwards. Posts that clear the bar earn wider reach. Posts that don't are basically dead within an hour.

The brutal part: only about 5% of posts that flop in the first 60 minutes ever recover to reach a broader audience.

"LinkedIn has pivoted entirely from rewarding 'time on platform at any cost' to prioritizing 'value delivered.'" (LinkBoost, LinkedIn Growth Hacking in 2026)

Company pages absorbed the heaviest damage.

Most of your followers no longer see your company posts.

If you run a company page, you've already felt this in your gut. Between 2024 and 2026, company-page organic reach fell apart. LinkedIn surfaces company posts to maybe 2 to 5% of followers on the initial pass. Across the whole feed, branded content fills barely 1 to 2% of what users actually scroll through.

Personal profiles are a completely different story. The same piece of content shared from a personal account pulls dramatically more distribution. LinkedIn's product team has been deliberately steering the feed toward individual voices for two years. They even locked advanced company analytics behind Premium plans.

"Personal profiles drive 2.75x more impressions and 5x more engagement compared to company pages." (Ordinal, LinkedIn Company Page Reach in January 2026)

Engagement pods are done.

LinkedIn named them as a target and built the systems to shut them down.

Gyanda Sachdeva, LinkedIn's VP of Product, said it publicly. The goal was to make pods "entirely ineffective." In February 2026, she confirmed that comments posted through third-party scripts or browser plugins would be stripped from the default comment view.

Lempod, the most popular pod tool, got pulled from the Chrome Web Store. 360Brew now maps what LinkedIn calls "Coordinated Activity Rings." When the same cluster of accounts engages within minutes of a post going live, every account in the cluster gets flagged. Shadow bans last 60 to 90 days. Repeat offenders face permanent suspension.

"One marketing director saw their average reach drop from 8,500 impressions to 340 overnight." (upGrowth, LinkedIn Algorithm 2026: 360Brew Update)

Hashtags stopped doing anything useful.

The feed now runs on semantic understanding, not tags.

LinkedIn removed the ability to follow hashtags. Dropped them from search entirely. Even renamed company-page hashtags to "Specialisms," which tells you how little the old tagging system matters now. The feed runs on natural language processing. It figures out what your post is about by reading the actual words. No label needed.

The data is striking. Posts with more than three hashtags now perform worse than posts with none. Overuse can trigger spam detection.

"Posts with more than 3 hashtags had 70% lower reach than posts with none." (Richard van der Blom, cited in upGrowth 360Brew analysis)

What actually drives distribution now.

Dwell time, saves, and comment threads. That's the short list.

The signals that matter shifted from visible reactions to deeper behavioural cues. LinkedIn is measuring whether your content genuinely held somebody's attention, not just whether they tapped a button.

Dwell time tracks how long somebody spends with your post on screen. Thirty seconds of real reading time outweighs 50 quick likes.

Saves are the strongest single signal. When somebody bookmarks your post for later, it tells the platform this content has reference value. Analysis of over three million posts found that saves carry 5x the reach impact of likes, and 2x the impact of comments.

Comment threads matter more than standalone comments. LinkedIn's system specifically looks for conversations where multiple people reply to each other, not just isolated responses aimed at the original poster. Multi-sentence comments carry far more weight than a clapping emoji or "Love this!"

"When someone saves your post, it drives 5x more reach than a like, and 2x more reach than a comment." (AuthoredUp, analysis of 3+ million LinkedIn posts)

Your profile is part of the ranking equation now.

360Brew checks your credentials before it distributes anything you write.

Most people skip right past this one. It might be the most consequential change in 360Brew. Before the system decides how far to push your post, it scans your profile. It checks the headline, the About section, and the work experience to see whether you're a credible source on the topic you just wrote about.

Post about marketing strategy with a headline that says "crypto investor"? The system sees a mismatch and limits your reach. Write a generic About section full of buzzwords? 360Brew can't figure out what you're an expert in, so it defaults to minimal distribution.

The fix isn't keyword stuffing. It's making your profile tell a clear, honest story about the problems you solve, and the people you solve them for.

"If your profile is vague, generic, or written like a motivational poster, you're making it harder for the platform to place you in the right conversations." (Converse Digital, The LinkedIn Algorithm Change You Need to Know in 2026)

Links to external websites will tank your post.

LinkedIn penalises anything that sends people off-platform.

Posts with an outbound link get roughly 60% less reach than identical posts without one. And the workaround everyone used to rely on, dropping the link in the first comment? That's penalised now too.

LinkedIn is building a walled garden. It wants users consuming content natively. If you have to share a URL, do it knowing you're trading reach for traffic. Sometimes that's the right trade. Just go in with your eyes open.

"Any post containing a link to a website outside LinkedIn sees roughly 60% less reach compared to identical posts without links." (River Blog, The 2026 LinkedIn Algorithm)

What to do about it.

Practical adjustments that work under the new system.

Narrow your topic. 360Brew categorises you by what you consistently write about. Posting about five different subjects dilutes the signal. Pick one lane and stay in it long enough for the system to learn what you're about. Usually 60 to 90 days of consistent publishing.

Write with real depth. Long-form content came back because 360Brew can evaluate it for substance. But length without insight gets penalised just as hard as shallow content. Show your thinking. Walk through a real situation you handled. Explain the trade-offs. Leave the reader knowing something they didn't before.

Rebuild your profile around your actual expertise. Your headline, About section, and experience entries have to line up with the topics you post about. This isn't optional. The system checks before it distributes your content.

Comment strategically every day. Posting three or four times a week gives you a content rhythm. Showing up in the comments of other people's posts, ten to fifteen times a day on relevant content, gets you in front of audiences who haven't found you yet. This compounds faster than publishing alone.

Make posts people will bookmark. Frameworks, decision trees, process breakdowns, step-by-step guides. Anything with enough reference value that a reader would hit save. That single action is the most powerful distribution lever on the platform right now.

Recalibrate what success looks like. Five hundred views from people who might actually buy what you sell are worth more than 50,000 views from random scrollers. The new system was designed for exactly that trade. Measure profile visits, connection requests from your target market, and inbound messages. Not impressions.

"The 2026 sweet spot is 3 to 4 highly valuable posts per week." (LinkBoost, LinkedIn Growth Hacking in 2026)
